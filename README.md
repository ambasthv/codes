The next variable assessed is Profit Before Taxes/(Total Assets-Total Liabilities), as seen in the Figure below:

Figure: Profit Before Taxes/(Total Assets-Total Liabilities) Bivariate Plot incl. Cap and Floor

As observed in the figure above, there is a general decrease in default rate as Profit Before Taxes/(Total Assets-Total Liabilities) increases. The most significant reduction in default rate is observed between approximately -1.34 and -0.71, with the actual default rate declining substantially from approximately 6.6% to 3.1%. Beyond approximately -0.71, the actual default rate continues to decline; however, the predicted default rate remains relatively flat and the actuals exhibit increasing variability at the lower default-rate levels.

Any obligor with a value below -1.34 would be considered to be at the riskiest level, given the substantially higher default rates observed at the lower end of the distribution. The same logic is applied to the less risky bucket for obligors above approximately -0.71, where the predicted default rate remains broadly flat and further differentiation in risk is less clearly supported by the observed relationship.

Ultimately, within the relevant buckets, a floor of -1.34 and a cap of -0.71 were selected as the lower and upper bounds of the variable.

As such, the cap and floor for the variable were established at -0.71 and -1.34, respectively. These caps and floors are reflected on the dark blue line in the figure above and we can observe that it captures the general trend of the behavior observed in the actuals (light blue line) while reducing the noise and limiting excessive differentiation at the extremes.
